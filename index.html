Patrol Report: what's in the air

Bars show each pollutant's sub-index on the same 0–300 scale NEA uses to build the overall PSI — whichever pollutant is highest becomes today's PSI number. NEA also tracks nitrogen dioxide (NO2), but doesn't publish it on this sub-index scale, so the patrol leaves it off this board to avoid comparing apples to durians.

Why grown-ups make a fuss about this

🫁Kids breathe faster than adults and their lungs are still growing — so the same hazy air affects you more.
🏃Running and playing hard makes you breathe deeper, pulling more haze particles into your lungs.
😮‍💨You can't always see or smell unhealthy air — that's why the patrol checks the PSI number instead of just looking outside.
🛡️On bad days, staying indoors or wearing a mask outside gives your lungs a break until the air clears up.
Live PSI readings from NEA via data.gov.sg. PSI bands: 0–50 Good, 51–100 Moderate, 101–200 Unhealthy, 201–300 Very Unhealthy, 301+ Hazardous. This is a fun helper, not an official health tool — for real haze advisories, check the NEA website or ask a grown-up. XP, ranks and badges are saved only on this device.
